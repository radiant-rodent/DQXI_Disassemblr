## Method (reproducible, all under `%TEMP%/opencode/n070/`)

1. Name map from `symbols.json`, `symbols_recovered.json`, `decomp/native_names.json` and all sibling
   `out/N_*.json` (`prep.py`).
2. Caller/callee index over all 18,367 readable-C files (`index.py`), plus per-function digests with
   signature, caller/callee names, strings, all `g_`/`s_` literals dereferenced from
   `original/exefs/code.decompressed.bin` (base 0x100000) (`detail.py`, `strs.py`).
3. **Vtable scan** (`vt.py`, `vtdump.py`): for every batch address, find every little-endian pointer to
   it in the image, then walk backwards/forwards to print the whole table with the already-named
   siblings. This is what turned ~55 "mystery" entries into class members (`Ui_NumberPopup_*`,
   `Ui_CriticalPopup_*`, `Field_BattleTrigger_Update`, `Ui_TextWindow_DrawToken`, …) and it is what
   proved `0x58F2BC = 0x782B54` is the Gfx_Scene vtable and `0x58F2C0 = 0x58D39C` the 0x9C-entry
   destructor.
4. Inline-string scan of each function's own byte range (`inline.py`) — these functions embed their
   literals right after the code, which is where `%s_ac`, `ReguralyShake`, `N_ALL`, `Narrow_Nut`,
   `Epath_035/036/037.btcs` and `_UNDEFINED` came from.
5. Bottom-up reading: small leaves first (grow/alloc helpers, dtors), then the classes around them.
6. For the text-command family I read the dispatcher `0x4E05F4` itself and mapped each command id to
   its handler, instead of guessing from the handler body alone.

## Honest gaps / not verified

- **Everything here is static.** No emulator run, no debugger, no dynamic confirmation of a single
  name. Behavioural claims come from bodies + call graphs + literals.
- **The two `Battle_Ui_MemberScreen_Update` giants (0x5940A4, 0x5990F8) and the party/row panels
  (0x59B05C…0x59D638)** are the weakest part: the objects are ~0xE000 bytes, sit in a vtable family
  whose slot 0 is `FieldParty_ResetMemberSlot` and whose neighbours mix `Battle_Ui_*` and
  `FieldParty_*` names from earlier lanes, and their screens cannot be identified without running the
  game. Their names describe the mechanism ("member screen", "row panel") and deliberately avoid
  claiming a specific menu. Confidence 0.4–0.5, all `maybe_`.
- **0x59B05C…0x59C6A4** were first drafted as `Ui_PartyListWindow_*`; that collided semantically with
  the existing `Ui_PartyListWindow_*` class at 0x4AE758/0x4AE780 and `Ui_PartyStatusWindow_*` at
  0x468070. They were renamed to `maybe_Battle_Ui_PartyPanel_*` / `maybe_Battle_Ui_RowPanel_*` to avoid
  grafting a second vocabulary onto existing classes. Whether they are battle or field panels is not
  proven.
- **0x592160** has no vtable reference anywhere in the image, so its class is inferred from layout only
  (0.4).
- **0x5959AC, 0x5961B8, 0x5965 14** are named from the dispatcher branch that reaches them, not from a
  complete reading of every local; the exact popup kind they spawn is uncertain (0.4).
- **0x58C230/0x58C2B8/0x58C370** are honest about what is known: record sizes 0x14/0x28/0x2C in a
  stack builder consumed by sprite creation. Whether the records are quads, particles or attachment
  keys is not proven (`Add14/Add40/Add44`, 0.42).
- **0x58FF38 / 0x5906D0** are copy-constructors with relative-pointer fixups; the struct identity
  (text word vs actor entry) rests on their single call sites (0.4).
- **0x591A0C / 0x591B8C** decompile with a damaged prologue; the visible tail (camera projection +
  NPC animation) is trusted, the argument meanings are not (0.4/0.35).
- `0x59F544` is a timed scroll/slide state machine; calling it the item-command scroll rests on its
  only caller (0.4).
- Two names reference helpers that are still `func_*` in the tree (`0x5E6590`, `0x665EBC`, `0x634D38`,
  `0x5E7848`); they are described by what they do, not by a merged symbol.
