## What the batch asked for

Name the 15 classes and every field of `orchestration/types/batches/S_004.json`
(subsystem `Anim`): `Anim_Object`, `Anim_InstanceList`, `Anim_SpriteList`,
`Anim_SequenceManager`, `Anim_Table`, `Anim_Instance`, `Anim_Clip`, `Anim_ClipSet`,
`Anim_TimeTrack`, `Anim_ActionSeq`, `Anim_ModelInstance`, `Anim_Resource`,
`Anim_Interp`, `Anim_ClipSlot`, `Anim_Sequence`.

## Method

For every class I read the readable C of its constructor first, then the
`update`/`draw`/`build` methods, and cross-checked the widths against the
disassembly in `asm/text/` whenever the pseudocode was damaged (`unaff_r*`,
`in_s0`, dropped float returns). `tools/fn.py` was used for the small vtable
thunks that have no readable file. No whole-tree walk or grep was performed.

## Decisions I made (the orchestrator was not available)

1. **Class names kept as generated.** The batch rules say to keep the generated
   `class`, so `Anim_InstanceList` stays even though the evidence shows it is a
   field timing/status helper (vtable 0x00775984 slot 2 is
   `maybe_Party_UpdateMemberHpDisplay` 0x004A70F8, slot 9 a scene timing
   calculator). The mismatch is stated in the class `summary` and `evidence`
   instead of renaming.
2. **Contaminated offsets are `unknown_*`, not guesses.** The batch is
   machine-generated and merges several distinct classes per name; where the only
   evidence lines belong to another object I wrote `unknown_XX` at 0.0 and said
   what the lines actually were.
3. **Overlapping offsets resolved to the narrower observed width.** The batch
   listed 0x24 and 0x26 as 4-byte fields in `Anim_Instance` and `Anim_ClipSet`,
   but `func_0029EC8C(clip, &self->f_24, &self->f_26)` writes two adjacent u16s,
   so both are `u16`/size 2. Same for `Anim_ClipSet` 0x2C/0x2D, which
   `Anim_ClipSet_Build` copies as two single bytes. This keeps the struct emitter
   from skipping a field.
4. **`Anim_Instance` 0x24/0x26 preferred over the rb-tree reading.** The ctor's
   `unaff_r5 = self->f_24` (damaged decompilation) would make +0x24 the parent
   link of the `_Rb_tree` header at +0x20; the clean `Anim_Instance_Update` call
   was preferred and the alternative is recorded in the field evidence.
5. **`Anim_Instance` 0x60..0x6C and 0x90..0xBC named structurally.** They are the
   first two of three 0x30-byte entries built by
   `std_ConstructArray(self+0x60, 0x006C7FC8, 0x30, 3)`; each entry is six 8-byte
   {value,value} pairs (entry ctor 0x006C7FC8 copies six `ldrd`/`strd` pairs).
   Named `maybe_refEntry0Fn0/Arg0/...` at 0.40 rather than inventing purposes.

## Useful finding for later batches

`Anim_Instance_ApplyTemplate` (0x002D1304) loads its defaults with
`ldr r0,[pc,#0x1e0]` at 0x002D1324, i.e. from the literal at **0x002D150C**, whose
value is **0x00725458**. Ghidra's `DAT_002d150c` is the literal slot, not the
data. The table at 0x00725458 holds code addresses (0x002D19F0, 0x002D1834,
0x002D1C98, 0x002D1AA8, 0x002D1D20, 0x002D1D34 = `Ui_Sprite_UpdateFromLayout`),
so it is a default-callback table, not a parameter template - the readable
function name `Anim_Instance_ApplyTemplate` and its doc ("copies the large default
parameter template") are misleading and should be revisited.

## Not verified

* No emulator run and no runtime check. Every offset here is static-analysis
  evidence only.
* The `maybe_*` names at 0.40-0.50 are hypotheses with the evidence quoted, not
  confirmed semantics. In particular the `maybe_refEntry*` block in
  `Anim_Instance` and the `maybe_desc*` block in `Anim_Resource` are structural
  descriptions, not purposes.
* `Anim_Instance` is a merge of two layouts (the 0x300-byte class with vtable
  0x00760568 and the 0x2A0-byte class allocated by
  `maybe_Anim_Instance_CreateForModel` 0x004A781C). The struct is emitted at
  0x300; the 0x2A0-byte class deserves its own entry in `structs.json`.
